EARTH JUKEBOX — TECHNICAL DOSSIER
NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Challenge "The Earth Information Jukebox"
================================================================================

1. WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Earth Jukebox turns NASA climate data (1880 to 2100) into music and into an
interactive, accessible 3D globe. Each climate indicator becomes an instrument:

  Global temperature ...... melody (higher, more dissonant and out of tune as it warms)
  CO2 ..................... low drone (distorts with more carbon dioxide)
  Arctic sea ice .......... bells (ring less as the ice shrinks)
  Sea level ............... waves (grow as the sea rises)

As the planet warms, the musical scale shifts from major to Locrian: the harmony
"falls apart". The project was designed from the start for blind and low-vision
users (voice narration, keyboard control, screen readers).

Up to the last measured year the values are OBSERVATIONS. After that, they are
PROJECTIONS from NASA's climate model under three emission scenarios
(SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP5-8.5).


2. WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM (ALL SOURCES ARE NASA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No value is typed by hand. Everything is downloaded and processed by reproducible
Python scripts (scripts/ folder). The origin of each series is recorded in
data/provenance.json, which is generated automatically.

2.1 Observed series (data/historical.js — generated by scripts/fetch_nasa_data.py)

  Global temperature (1880–2025)
    NASA GISS — GISTEMP v4, global annual anomaly (1951–1980 baseline)
    https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/GLB.Ts+dSST.csv

  CO2 (1880–2014)
    NASA GISS — global concentrations used by the ModelE climate model (CMIP6)
    https://portal.nccs.nasa.gov/GISS_modelE/modelE_input_data/GHG.CMIP6.1-2014.txt

  Arctic sea ice (1979–2017, September extent)
    NASA Goddard Space Flight Center — Cryospheric Sciences Lab
    https://earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/neptune/files/NH_IceExt_Monthly_1978-2017.txt
    Before 1979 there is no satellite record: the app says "no satellite".

  Sea level (1993–2025)
    NASA JPL / NASA GSFC (NASA Sea Level Change program) — global mean sea level
    from NASA-SSH satellite altimetry (TOPEX/Poseidon, Jason-1/2/3, Sentinel-6).
    Open file published by the NASA scientists (Willis, Hamlington, Fournier) and
    referenced by NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5611):
    https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7702315
    Before 1993 there is no satellite record: the app says "no satellite"
    (no values are invented).

2.2 Projections 2015–2100 (data/projections.js — generated by scripts/fetch_nasa_data.py)

  Temperature and Arctic sea ice
    NASA GISS-E2.1-G climate model (CMIP6, member r1i1p1f2), variables "tas"
    (temperature) and "siconca" (sea ice), via the NASA Center for Climate
    Simulation (NCCS):
    https://portal.nccs.nasa.gov/datashare/giss_cmip6/
    - Temperature: area-weighted global mean; anchored so that the model's
      2015–2024 mean matches the GISTEMP observations.
    - Sea ice: September extent (cells with >= 15% ice), bias-corrected against
      the GSFC observations for 1979–2014.

  CO2
    NASA GISS — ModelE forcing files for each scenario
    https://portal.nccs.nasa.gov/GISS_modelE/modelE_input_data/GHG.CMIP6.2015-2500.<scenario>.txt

  Sea level
    Semi-empirical model (Rahmstorf, 2007) driven by the GISS model temperature of
    each scenario. It starts at the current rate measured by NASA (4.4 mm/year,
    from the same NASA file) and reaches in 2100 the median of the NASA Sea Level
    Projection Tool (0.44 / 0.56 / 0.77 m, the same values as IPCC AR6 table 9.9):
    https://sealevel.nasa.gov/ipcc-ar6-sea-level-projection-tool
    The conversion from the 1995–2014 baseline to 1993 uses NASA's own observed series.

2.3 Visual layers on the globe (assets/layers/ — generated by scripts/build_layers.py)

  Temperature anomaly map ............ NASA GISTEMP (1200 km grid) + GISS-E2.1-G
  Real clouds (16 days, March 2024) .. NASA GIBS — VIIRS (Suomi NPP and NOAA-20),
                                       Corrected Reflectance (true color)
  Vegetation (NDVI) .................. NASA GIBS — MODIS Terra, monthly NDVI
  Fires .............................. NASA GIBS — MODIS Terra+Aqua Thermal Anomalies
  Winds .............................. NASA GISS-E2.1-G, 10 m wind (uas/vas),
                                       decadal mean per scenario
  Ozone hole ......................... NASA Ozone Watch (GSFC) — TOMS and OMI
  Ocean currents ..................... NASA GISS-E2.1-G sea surface height (zos),
                                       converted to geostrophic currents (1985–2014 mean)
  GIBS: https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov
  Ozone Watch: https://ozonewatch.gsfc.nasa.gov

2.4 Base textures of the globe (assets/textures/ — generated by scripts/build_textures.py)

  Daytime surface ...... NASA Blue Marble Next Generation (topography and bathymetry,
                         May 2004) — NASA Earth Observatory
  Relief ............... NASA Blue Marble Next Generation, topography
                         (used for the 3D relief and the flooding calculation)
  Water mask ........... NASA MODIS Terra Land/Water Mask (via GIBS); south of 60°S
                         (outside MODIS coverage) it uses the topography above
  City lights .......... NASA Black Marble 2016 (VIIRS) — lights only

  The only texture that is not data: assets/textures/waves.png, a mathematically
  generated wave texture (visual effect for the water).


3. HOW THE CODE WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Static web application: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no server and no install.
It works offline once loaded. 3D uses three.js (bundled in the project); sound is
synthesized in real time with the Web Audio API.

3.1 Overall flow

  [Python scripts] --> download NASA data --> generate data/*.js and assets/
          |
          v
  [browser]  data.js joins observations + projection of the chosen scenario
          |
          +--> audio.js  (music)        +--> globe.js (3D globe and layers)
          +--> chart.js  (chart)        +--> main.js  (interface, timeline)
          +--> a11y.js   (narration, keyboard, accessibility)

3.2 Main files

  index.html ............. the application page (interface over the globe)
  intro.html ............. welcome screen: language choice and spoken introduction
  css/style.css .......... styling (rem units, high contrast, reduced motion)

  js/data.js ............. DATA LAYER. Joins the observed series with the scenario's
                           projection. PCHIP interpolation (smooth curves, no jumps).
                           The projection connects to the last measured value with
                           no step. Before the first measurement the series has no
                           value (null) and the app says "no satellite".
  js/audio.js ............ SOUND ENGINE. Each dataset drives an instrument; the
                           temperature chooses the musical mode (major to Locrian).
  js/globe.js ............ 3D GLOBE (three.js + GLSL shaders):
                           - real relief displacing the vertices;
                           - coastal flooding from the relief (vertically exaggerated
                             so it is visible at global scale);
                           - sea ice edge computed from the measured extent;
                           - per-year layers using "atlases" (one image with one
                             frame per year), with smooth transitions between years;
                           - clouds: real days morphing into each other, carried by
                             the NASA model's wind;
                           - winds as a fluid: 16,000 particles computed on the
                             graphics card leave trails (streamlines);
                           - ocean with moving waves and currents.
  js/chart.js ............ chart of the four series (past solid, future dashed)
  js/events.js ........... historical events on the timeline and their impacts
  js/main.js ............. interface: timeline, play/pause, scenarios, panels,
                           "Tell the story" mode
  js/a11y.js ............. accessibility (see section 4)
  js/i18n.js ............. Portuguese and English (text, narration, screen reader labels)
  js/vision.js ........... "listen to images": reads color, brightness and motion of
                           images and videos (e.g. NASA visualizations) and turns
                           them into sound
  js/score.js ............ plays sheet music (MusicXML, MIDI and score images)
  js/narrator-*.js ....... recorded English narration and offline backup voice

  scripts/fetch_nasa_data.py .. downloads NASA series -> data/historical.js,
                                data/projections.js, data/provenance.json
  scripts/build_layers.py ..... builds the globe layers -> assets/layers/, data/layers.js
  scripts/build_textures.py ... builds the base textures -> assets/textures/
  scripts/serve.py ............ local server to run the project

3.3 Timeline
  When Play is pressed, each year lasts one "beat". The year's values feed the music,
  the globe and the chart at the same time. In "Tell the story" mode, the music
  pauses at 25 events (e.g. the Keeling curve, the ozone hole, the Paris Agreement)
  and a voice tells what happened, the impact on Earth and the data measured that
  year. After 2025, future milestones are built from the chosen scenario's projections.


4. ACCESSIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Voice narration always on when the page opens: every button says what it does
    and its current state.
  - Spoken welcome screen: any key tells how to choose the language (P / E).
  - Everything works with the keyboard only; shortcuts for every function.
  - Works with screen readers (NVDA, Narrator): ARIA states on every control.
  - "Describe" button: reads the year, values, harmony and active layers.
  - Interface sounds in the same key as the music (you can "hear" where you are).
  - Text size, high contrast, reduced motion, hide interface, full screen.
  - Three voices in order: recorded narration (English) -> browser voice -> offline voice.


5. TRANSPARENCY AND LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Projections come from ONE simulation of ONE model (GISS-E2.1-G). Year-to-year
    variation belongs to the model; it is not a forecast of each specific year.
  - Flooding and the sea ice edge use real data with visual exaggeration so they
    are visible at global scale (stated in the interface).
  - Projected sea level is semi-empirical (Rahmstorf 2007), calibrated to the NASA
    Sea Level Projection Tool; it is not a direct model output.
  - Ocean currents represent the 1985–2014 mean climate.
  - The growth of city lights over the years is an illustrative curve (NASA only
    has recent satellite night imagery); the location of the lights is real
    (Black Marble 2016).
  - The texts of historical events and impacts are summaries written by the team
    from public facts; the numbers quoted come from the data above.
  - The English narration of the events was recorded with an AI synthetic voice.


6. HOW TO RUN AND REPRODUCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Run:        double-click run.bat (opens http://localhost:8013)
              or: python scripts/serve.py
  Online:     published as a static website (GitHub Pages).

  Reproduce the data (Python 3 + libraries in scripts/requirements.txt):
      python scripts/fetch_nasa_data.py
      python scripts/build_layers.py
      python scripts/build_textures.py
  Downloads are cached in data/cache/ (outside the repository). If a source is
  offline, the script keeps the previous values and reports why.
